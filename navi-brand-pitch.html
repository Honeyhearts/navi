Navi is a pre-configured AI companion for people who are too busy running their business to set up another app. You tell Navi what's falling through the cracks. It handles the follow-ups, the reminders, the messages that pile up while you're doing the actual work. Then it gets out of the way.

You chose your work because you're good at it. Navi exists so you can keep doing it.

The Brand

Extended intelligence for everyday people

We built the internet to connect us. We built smartphones to put the world in our hands. Somewhere along the way, the tools we made to serve human life started consuming it instead — and most of us just adapted, accepting an ever-growing pile of digital overhead as the price of being alive and working in the modern world. Navi exists to push back on that.

"Most people aren't drowning in their work. They're drowning in the administration of their work. Those are different problems."

The barber who's great at his craft spends two hours a day on his phone chasing no-shows. The solo attorney who built a practice to help families is buried in intake paperwork. The tattoo artist who could be drawing is fielding booking DMs at midnight. These aren't productivity problems. They're dignity problems. People who started something because they were good at something shouldn't have to become full-time administrators to survive doing it.

Navi isn't AI that thinks for you. It doesn't make decisions you should make. It doesn't replace your judgment, your craft, or your relationships. It takes care of the digital layer — the reminders, the follow-ups, the scheduling, the messages that pile up between the real moments of your day — so you can bring more of yourself to the things that actually matter. That's what we mean by extended intelligence: not replacing the human, but freeing them.

Technology should serve connection, not abstraction. The measure of a good tool isn't what it can do — it's how present it lets you be when it's done doing it.

There's a reason Navi arrives every morning. Not as a notification — as a navigator. It's already looked at your day. It already knows what needs attention. It's already drafted the message you haven't sent. You wake up, you read three lines, and you're free to go be wherever you actually need to be. That's the product. Not automation — presence. Not efficiency — dignity. A little more of your day returned to you, consistently, without setup, without a learning curve, without another app you'll stop using in two weeks.

Navi doesn't moralize about your inbox or optimize your habits. It knows what it's like to have forty tabs open in your brain. It doesn't lecture you about it. It just quietly helps you close a few.
