## The Person
Darnell started his cleaning company 5 years ago with one goal: build something good enough to pay his people a real wage with real benefits. He's at 5 cleaners now. He can almost see the horizon where that's possible. But the operations — rescheduling, client complaints, coverage when someone calls in sick — are a second full-time job that grinds him down before he can get to the work that actually grows the business.

## Core Pain
Every morning starts with a fire. A client rescheduled, a cleaner called in, a new inquiry he hasn't responded to, a complaint about last Tuesday. By the time he gets through the calls and texts, half the morning is gone. The irony: the admin overhead is exactly what's keeping him from growing to the revenue level where he could hire help.

## What Falls Through the Cracks
- Responding to new client inquiries fast enough to win the job
- Rescheduling when a cleaner calls out sick (finding coverage, notifying the client)
- Following up after first cleans to get reviews and lock in recurring bookings
- Tracking which clients are on recurring schedules vs. one-time
- Sending reminders to clients whose regular slot is coming up
- Collecting and responding to complaints before they become Yelp reviews

## How Navi Helps
Navi runs his operations layer — it surfaces the day's schedule issues, drafts client communications, handles first-response to new inquiries, and flags the complaints that need Darnell personally. The goal: Darnell spends his mornings building the business, not dispatching it.

Want me to contact him and notify the Hendersons? Also: 2 new inquiries came in overnight, I've drafted responses to both. And you have 4 first-time cleans from last week that haven't been followed up for reviews yet.
