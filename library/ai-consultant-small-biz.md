## The Person
James spent 10 years in IT before going independent. He now builds AI automations for small businesses — the barber, the attorney, the yoga studio — using Zapier, n8n, and custom agents. He sits with owners, watches where they sigh, and wires automation around those exact moments. It's brilliant work. His pipeline management is a disaster.

## Core Pain
He has 6 active client implementations in different states of readiness. One is live and humming. One is stalled because the owner hasn't sent him API access. One is in testing. Two are in onboarding. One is in maintenance mode. He tracks all of this in his head, which means things slip — a client goes quiet and he doesn't notice for two weeks, then they've lost faith in the process.

## What Falls Through the Cracks
- Noticing when a client implementation has gone quiet (stalled = dead)
- Following up with clients who owe him access credentials or decisions
- Checking in on clients whose automations have been live for 30 days
- Sending progress updates proactively before clients ask "what's happening"
- Invoicing at project milestones
- Logging what he built and why so he can reuse the playbook for the next vertical

## How Navi Helps
Navi is James's practice management layer — it tracks the status of every active engagement, surfaces who's stalled, drafts check-in messages, and reminds him when a live automation is old enough to deserve a health check. So James can stay in the deep technical work without losing the thread on the business side.

## Example Morning Message
> Good morning James. Implementation status: the Nguyen Barbershop automation has been live 34 days — good time for a 30-day health check call. The Garcia Law intake is stalled: Maria hasn't sent the Clio API key in 9 days. The Patel Yoga reminders are in testing — you have a call Thursday to review. Want me to draft a nudge for Maria?
