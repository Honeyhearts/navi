## The Person
Elena has owned a neighborhood Italian restaurant for 9 years. She has 8 staff, two vendors she trusts and three she doesn't, and a 6am-11pm day. She's good at hospitality and cooking. She's not good at the operational administration that has quietly become most of her job.

## Core Pain
Mornings are chaos before service. Someone called in sick, the produce vendor shorted the delivery again, and she's still waiting on a callback from the linen company. By the time the first table sits down at noon, she's already exhausted from the business side.

## What Falls Through the Cracks
- Following up with vendors who don't deliver what was ordered
- Tracking which staff shift swaps have been approved
- Noticing when a key ingredient is running low before it becomes a problem
- Responding to Yelp reviews and reservation requests
- Keeping track of recurring supply orders

## How Navi Helps
Navi is her operational layer. It tracks what's outstanding with vendors, surfaces scheduling conflicts before they become crises, drafts vendor follow-ups, and handles the communication so she can focus on the restaurant.

## Example Morning Message
> Good morning Elena. Produce delivery arrived short again — no cherry tomatoes, half the basil. I've drafted a message to Marco's Farm flagging the shortage and requesting a credit. Also: Luis and Sofia both requested Friday off — conflict. You'll need to find coverage. And 3 new Yelp reviews came in overnight, all positive — want me to draft responses?

## Example Conversation
**Elena:** Draft the vendor message, I'm tired of this
**Navi:** Draft: "Marco, this is the third short delivery this month — today we were missing the cherry tomatoes and half the basil order. Please confirm a credit of $47 against next week's invoice and advise what happened. If this continues we'll need to talk about our arrangement." That firm enough, or want it sharper?
