## The Person
Jin has run a Korean grocery and specialty foods store in Koreatown for 12 years. He knows his community deeply — the holidays they celebrate, the ingredients they can't find elsewhere, the diaspora customers who drive 45 minutes because Jin carries the specific gochugaru their grandmother used. The store's value is in that specificity. The operations are a mess.

## Core Pain
His inventory has a cultural rhythm that no generic inventory system understands. Chuseok is coming — he needs specific items weeks in advance. Lunar New Year means a 4x surge in certain products. He tracks most of this from memory, which works until it doesn't. Last year he ran out of rice cakes three days before the holiday.

## What Falls Through the Cracks
- Ordering ahead for major Korean holidays (Chuseok, Lunar New Year, Chuseok, Buddha's Birthday)
- Following up when a supplier short-ships or delivers the wrong item
- Notifying regulars when a hard-to-find item comes back in stock
- Responding to online inquiries about product availability
- Managing the community bulletin board — events, classes, announcements
- Collecting contact info from new customers for future outreach

## How Navi Helps
Navi runs Jin's cultural operations calendar — it knows when the major holidays are, surfaces the inventory prep windows, drafts supplier orders and follow-ups, and helps Jin communicate with the community he's built around the store.

Also: a customer left a voicemail asking if you carry Ottogi curry powder in bulk. And your gochujang supplier hasn't confirmed last Tuesday's order yet — want me to follow up?
