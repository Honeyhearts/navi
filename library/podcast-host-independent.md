## The Person
Marcus hosts a business podcast with 14,000 listeners. He's been doing it for 2 years, puts out 2 episodes a week, and does everything himself — booking guests, scheduling, prep research, editing notes, publishing, social promotion. It's 20 hours a week of work surrounding a 2-hour interview.

## Core Pain
The guest pipeline is chaotic. People say yes then go quiet. Follow-ups get lost in email threads. He has a list of people he wants to reach out to that he's been meaning to get to for two months. Episode prep is rushed because he didn't build a system.

## What Falls Through the Cracks
- Following up with guests who agreed but haven't scheduled
- Reaching out to the "dream guest" list he never gets to
- Building episode prep briefs before interviews
- Publishing-day checklists (show notes, social posts, email blast)
- Tracking which guests have been on and which were pitched but didn't respond

## How Navi Helps
Navi is his production coordinator — it manages the guest pipeline, drafts outreach emails, builds prep briefs from research, and runs the episode launch checklist so Marcus shows up to every interview prepared.

## Example Morning Message
> Good morning Marcus. Episode 89 drops today — social posts are queued (review them in the folder). Two pipeline items: Dr. Reyes said yes two weeks ago and hasn't scheduled — worth a follow-up. And you mentioned wanting to pitch Sahil Bloom — I've drafted an outreach email if you want to review it. Interview tomorrow: you have 3 hours of prep time blocked at 2pm.

## Example Conversation
**Marcus:** What's the prep brief look like for tomorrow?
**Navi:** Here's a draft: Guest is founder of [Company], $12M raised, known for her essay on building without VC. Recent interview themes: operator vs. visionary tension, hiring early. Suggested angles: what she'd do differently, the moment she almost quit. Anything you want me to add?
