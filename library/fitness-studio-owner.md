## The Person
Dana opened her boutique fitness studio 3 years ago — a mix of HIIT, barre, and mobility classes. She has 3 instructors and about 85 active members. The economics only work if members stay. Industry research shows the fitness industry loses as many as 50% of members within the first year (Spacebring, 2024). Dana's churn is lower than that but high enough to force her to run constant promotions to replace the people she's losing.

## Core Pain
She has no system for noticing when someone is drifting. A member goes from 4 classes a week to 2, then 1, then zero — and Dana only realizes they've quit when she stops seeing them. By then, re-engagement is much harder than it would have been at the first sign of drop-off.

## What Falls Through the Cracks
- Noticing when member attendance drops (the early warning sign)
- Reaching out before someone fully quits
- Welcoming new members with a proper onboarding sequence
- Announcing schedule changes before members show up to a canceled class
- Celebrating member milestones (100th class, 6-month anniversary)
- Staff scheduling conflicts when an instructor is sick

## How Navi Helps
Navi watches Dana's membership like a retention radar. It surfaces attendance drop-offs, drafts re-engagement messages at the right window, handles new member onboarding communication, and manages the operational alerts — so Dana can focus on building the community, not managing the spreadsheet.

## Example Morning Message
> Good morning Dana. Retention alert: 8 members have attended fewer than 2 classes in the past 3 weeks — down from their previous average. 3 of those have been members for 6+ months (higher value to re-engage). Want me to draft personalized outreach for those 3? Also: Instructor Alex called out sick — she had the 6am and 9am tomorrow. You'll need coverage. And two new members joined this week who haven't received their welcome sequence yet.

## Example Conversation
**Dana:** Yes on the re-engagement outreach, and send the welcome sequence to the new members
**Navi:** For the three at-risk long-term members I'll draft personal messages that reference their history: "[Name] — noticed you haven't been in a while and wanted to check in. We miss you. Is there anything about the schedule or class mix that's not working for you?" For new members, sending the welcome sequence: intro email, first-week tips, and a check-in on day 7. On it.
